Political parties devote substantial resources to campaigning, yet we know little about the electoral effects of the campaigns they actually deploy. An identical campaign may also affect voters differently depending on their political preferences and the strategic choices they face in their constituency. I study the UK Labour Party’s 2024 general election campaign using internal data on constituency-level resource allocation. Labour’s allocation rule generates two discontinuities that allow me to estimate the causal effects of mobilizing its full set of campaign resources in both swing and stronghold seats. The campaign increased Labour’s victory margin by 7.3 percentage points in strongholds but had no comparable effect in swing seats. Individual-level data indicate that left-leaning voters consolidate behind Labour in both settings. The right-wing electoral bloc fragments in strongholds as voters shift toward the far right, but consolidates behind the Conservatives in swing seats. The electoral effect of campaigning is therefore endogenous to the competitiveness of a seat, reflecting not only support for the campaigning party but also how its opponents’ supporters respond.
