I study partisan bias in news coverage by measuring the systematic omission of politically relevant stories. I use large language models to match nearly one million chyrons from Fox News, CNN, MSNBC, and the BBC to a universe of more than 140,000 Associated Press articles, allowing me to measure which available stories each network chooses to cover. I find substantial partisan differences in story selection. Fox News is much less likely to report political stories harmful to Republicans, while CNN and MSNBC exhibit a less pronounced pattern for stories harmful to Democrats. I test whether this bias is demand-driven using NASCAR races as an instrument for Fox News viewership. Despite a substantial decline in viewership and a shift in audience composition, I find no meaningful change in Fox News content. I also use internal communications released in the Dominion litigation to test whether Rupert Murdoch's instructions about specific stories affected subsequent coverage, finding suggestive but inconclusive evidence of his influence. Together, the results indicate that audience demand alone does not explain these differences in coverage.
