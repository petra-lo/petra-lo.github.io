I study partisan bias in news story selection, asking whether cable news networks selectively omit politically relevant stories and what drives these differences in coverage. I find substantial partisan differences in story selection. Fox News is much less likely to report political stories harmful to Republicans, while CNN and MSNBC exhibit a less pronounced pattern for stories harmful to Democrats. I test whether this bias is demand-driven using NASCAR races as an instrument for Fox News viewership. Despite NASCAR driving a substantial decline in viewership and a shift in audience composition, I detect no meaningful change in Fox News content across the dimensions I measure. I also use internal communications released in the Dominion litigation to test whether Rupert Murdoch’s instructions about specific stories affected subsequent coverage, finding suggestive but inconclusive evidence of his influence. Together, the results suggest that audience demand alone does not explain the partisan differences in story selection I observe.
