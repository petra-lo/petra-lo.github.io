In proportional systems with electoral thresholds, competition within an ideological bloc can mechanically reduce its representation by splitting support across party lists. We examine whether within-bloc competition also changes voter behavior. To study this, we ran a randomized canvassing experiment with the Social Democratic Party across precincts covering 1.4 million people in Croatia’s 2024 parliamentary election. The party’s coalition talks with the Greens failed, and the parties ran separate campaigns. Conditional on the Greens campaigning in a precinct, we estimate the effect of randomly assigned Social Democratic canvassing. The random assignment varies one component of within-bloc competition for the same voters. To determine where the Greens campaigned, we geolocate more than 400 campaign photographs posted on social media, matching visible features in each image to Google Street View. In these areas, Social Democratic canvassing increases the incumbent Christian Democrats’ vote share by 0.6–1.0 percentage points and reduces turnout by a similar amount. The results identify an additional cost of within-bloc competition through voter demobilization.
